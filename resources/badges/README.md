# OCL Badges

## What an OCL badge means

An OCL badge shows that you have put community leadership into practice.

It means you explored an idea from the OCL framework, used it in a real community, and reflected on what happened. The evidence does not need to be polished or perfect. It needs to show what you tried, what you noticed, and what you learned.

Badges are not exams, rankings, or proof that someone is a “better” community leader. Community work is far too human and complicated for that.

They recognise skills practised in the real world, then shared so others can learn from them too.

Each badge is connected to an OCL module and to the path you took through it. Digital badges are issued through Badgecraft and can be shared on LinkedIn, in an email signature, on your website, or wherever you keep a record of your community work.

## How the badge system works

OCL has six modules, and each module has three learning paths. Together, they form a collection of 18 badges.

The **module** shows what part of community leadership you worked on.

For example:

*Module 01 - Experience is the Community*

Badges from the same module share a visual identity, so they are easy to recognise as part of the same family.

The **path** shows how you completed the work:

* **Self-guided**: you worked through the module independently and applied it in your own community
* **Workshop**: you took part in a live session and completed the module activities
* **Mentor**: you guided other people through the module and supported their learning

The same module can be experienced in more than one way. You may earn all three badges over time as your role grows from participant, to practitioner, to mentor.

## How to earn a badge

Choose the module you are working on, then follow the path that reflects what you actually did.

### Self-guided

Work through the module in your own time, complete its worksheet or canvas, and test at least one idea in a community you care about.

### Workshop

Take part in a live OCL workshop, complete the session activities, and leave with an observation, decision, or practical action for your community.

### Mentor

Guide others through the module, create space for their reflection, review the evidence they share, and record what you learned from facilitating the process.

Each path recognises a different kind of work. Choose the badge that tells the honest story of how you completed the module.

## How to claim your badge

You can find the badge you need in either of two ways:

* Follow the badge link provided at the end of the module you completed.
* Visit the [OCL project on Badgecraft](https://www.badgecraft.eu/en/projects/23156) and choose the badge for your module and path.

Once you have opened the correct badge:

1. Select **Get this badge**.
2. Log in to Badgecraft, or create a Badge Wallet account.
3. Add the evidence requested for your path.
4. Submit your badge application for review.

For example, someone who completed the live workshop for Module 01 would choose:

*Module 01 - Workshop - Experience is the Community*

Make sure the module and path match the work you completed. Once your submission is approved, the badge will appear in your Badge Wallet.

## Digital and physical badges

Every OCL badge is available digitally through Badgecraft.

Your Badge Wallet gives you a portable record of what you have completed. You can share individual badges or your wider collection on LinkedIn, in your email signature, on your personal website, or as part of a community leadership portfolio.

Some in-person OCL workshops also include a physical badge. You may receive this at the workshop, while the digital badge is issued through Badgecraft after your participation and evidence have been confirmed.

The physical badge is something to carry. The digital badge carries the story behind it.

## What counts as evidence

Evidence is not about producing a perfect report. It is simply a way to show that you engaged with the module and connected it to real community practice.

Different paths ask for different forms of evidence:

| Path        | What you might submit                                                                      |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Self-guided | A reflection, completed activity, or artefact from your own community practice             |
| Workshop    | An activity output, completed canvas, or action identified during the session              |
| Mentor      | Facilitator notes, reflections from the session, and confirmation of the work you reviewed |

Evidence could include:

* A completed canvas or worksheet
* A summary of a community audit
* Notes or outputs from a workshop
* A retrospective
* A template or checklist you created
* A small change you tested in your community
* A before-and-after account of an improvement
* A short written, audio, or video reflection
* A link to something you created or changed
* A photograph or scan of handwritten work

Choose the form that best fits the work you did. Community leadership does not always happen inside neat documents, and your evidence does not have to either.

## What happens to your evidence

Your evidence is reviewed to confirm that it matches the badge criteria. It is not graded for writing quality, design, or how impressive the outcome appears.

Trying something that did not work still counts as practice, provided you can reflect honestly on what happened.

Before submitting, remove private information, personal details, or anything shared with you in confidence. When possible, describe the learning without identifying individual community members.

The goal is not to prove that everything went well.

The goal is to show that you participated, took action, and learned from what happened.
